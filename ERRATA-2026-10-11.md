## 2. What went wrong in the analysis

### 2.1 Ramsey: sampled at the Nyquist limit
- The hardware sweep has 31 points over 0–60 µs, a 2 µs step. That puts the Nyquist
  limit at **250 kHz, exactly the programmed detuning**.
- Any real qubit frame offset δ therefore folds back below 250 kHz. A fringe at
  250 − δ and one at 250 + δ produce identical samples.
- Fits started at 227.5 kHz and at 272.5 kHz give **identical residuals**. Only |δ| is measurable.
- On this grid the frozen estimator also has a blind zone: true fringes at 245 and
  255 kHz both read as exactly 250.000 kHz.
- The simulation selftest uses 61 points (1 µs step), so it never exercised the hardware grid.

What the data does show is a **real physical frequency offset** between the qubit and
its calibrated drive frame. That is a genuine metrology result, and the closest thing
in this repo to what BASE measures.

### 2.2 T2 echo: the estimator reads high on the hardware sweep
- `fit_exponential` searches the baseline over `linspace(0, y.min(), 41)`.
- With an echo floor near 0.5, the grid step (~0.013) is comparable to the decay tail
  still left at 400 µs.
- Noiseless synthetic results on the exact hardware grid: true 80 µs reads **+32.8%**,
  90 µs reads **+19.2%**, 110 µs reads +0.5%, 150 µs reads +6.6%. The bias does not
  shrink steadily as T2 grows.
- The selftest has no pass/fail check on T2, so this went unseen.
- The amend-A fix was validated for T1 (floor near 0) and then reused for T2 (floor
  near 0.5) without re-checking.

### 2.3 T1: model-dependent, not estimator-biased
- On ideal single-exponential data the frozen T1 fit is unbiased (−0.2% to +0.1%).
  The amend-A fix works.
- The hardware decays are **not single-exponential**: reduced χ² is 4.3 for rep 1 and
  9–12 for reps 2–4, and the fitted floor is about 0.09 against a readout floor near 0.02.
- An unweighted log-linear fit and a weighted nonlinear fit therefore disagree by
  about 15%. That disagreement is the honest systematic uncertainty. The
  "± 1.1 µs" spread across reps hides it.

### 2.4 Rabi: the estimator cannot resolve the period
- On the 21-point grid, the zero-padded FFT returns **6.187 rad** for true periods
  of both 6.283 and 6.347 rad.
- So "matches the predicted −1.54% to the digit" is a property of the estimator.
  It is not evidence about the hardware.
- Separately, IBM compiles `Rx(θ)` into virtual-Z rotations around fixed √X pulses.
  The sweep varies a frame phase, not the drive amplitude.
- This is a valid end-to-end plumbing test, as OPERATIONS-PLAN framed it. It is not
  a Rabi measurement in BASE's sense.

## 3. The BASE comparison is withdrawn

The frozen script computes BASE's ratio as 50 s divided by an assumed 1 ms "spin-flip
timescale", giving about 5×10⁴. The *Nature* manuscript (Latacz et al., Nature 644,
64–68 (2025)) reports:
- a Rabi frequency of **Ω₀/2π ≈ 50 mHz**, so a π-pulse lasts about 10 s;
- a drive time of **16 s** for the optimized scan;
- an effective coherence time of **50.2(4.8) s**;
- a linewidth of **156(4) mHz FWHM**.

So BASE performs **a few coherent operations per coherence time**, not tens of
thousands. The transmon, at about 10³, is in the *opposite* regime. The claim "same
qualitative regime — the ratio is the analogy's payload" is **retracted**.

BASE's figure of merit is resolution: a narrow linewidth from long coherent
interrogation, not an operation count. Any replacement comparison, such as
Fourier-limited linewidth or fractional frequency resolution, is a new claim. It
should be preregistered before it is published.

The transmon side also rests on an **assumed** 60 ns gate time. The real `sx`
duration for the qubit actually used comes from `reanalysis/recover_qubits.py`. For
reference, Qiskit's simulated ibm_fez snapshot lists 24 ns. If that holds for the
real qubit, the ratio would be about 3.7×10³.

## 6. Root causes, for the next frozen version

1. **The simulation does not test what runs on hardware.** The hardware path rebuilds
   its circuits inline instead of calling `ramsey_circuit` and the other builders, and
   the selftest uses different sweep grids. One set of builders and one set of sweep
   constants should serve both paths.
2. **Estimators were validated only where they cannot fail**: noiseless data at the
   programmed value. Validate across a range of true values on the exact hardware grid,
   including off-nominal frequencies, and add a pass/fail check on T2.
3. **No fit uncertainty or goodness-of-fit output.** Report ±1σ and reduced χ², and
   flag when the model does not fit.
4. **Nothing recorded the transpiler's choices.** Save the physical qubit, the actual
   delays after rounding to the hardware clock, and raw counts (not only P1) at fetch time.
