**± conventions.** *Fit σ* is the statistical error from a single fit (shot noise
only). *Sample SD* is the spread across repeated runs. A *range* or *spread* is how
far the value moves between the specific fit models or estimators tried: a sensitivity

figure above says which it is.

## 2. What went wrong in the analysis

### 2.1 Ramsey: sampled at the Nyquist limit
- The hardware sweep has 31 points over 0–60 µs, a 2 µs step. That puts the Nyquist
  limit at **250 kHz, exactly the programmed detuning**.
- Any real qubit frame offset δ therefore folds back below 250 kHz. A fringe at
  250 − δ and one at 250 + δ produce identical samples.
- Fits started at 227.5 kHz and at 272.5 kHz give **identical residuals**. Only |δ| is measurable.
- On this grid the frozen estimator also has a blind zone: true fringes at 245 and
  255 kHz both read as exactly 250.000 kHz.
- The simulation selftest uses 61 points (1 µs step), so it never exercised the hardware grid.

What the data does show is an **apparent frequency offset** between the qubit and its
calibrated drive frame, of about 21–23 kHz. A non-zero offset appears in every fit
model tried, so it is stable across those choices; that does not prove it is physical.
Its exact value depends on the model, and its
cause (qubit frequency drift, coupling to a neighbour or a defect, or something else)
is not identified (§9). Even so, it is the closest thing in this repo to what BASE
measures: a frequency read from coherent fringes.

### 2.2 T2 echo: the estimator reads high on the hardware sweep
- `fit_exponential` searches the baseline over `linspace(0, y.min(), 41)`.
- With an echo floor near 0.5, the grid step (~0.013) is comparable to the decay tail
  still left at 400 µs.
- Noiseless synthetic results on the exact hardware grid: true 80 µs reads **+32.8%**,
  90 µs reads **+19.2%**, 110 µs reads +0.5%, 150 µs reads +6.6%. The bias does not
  shrink steadily as T2 grows.
- The selftest has no pass/fail check on T2, so this went unseen.
- The amend-A fix was validated for T1 (floor near 0) and then reused for T2 (floor
  near 0.5) without re-checking.

### 2.3 T1: model-dependent, not estimator-biased
- On ideal single-exponential data the frozen T1 fit is unbiased (−0.2% to +0.1%).
  The amend-A fix works.
- The hardware decays are **not single-exponential**: reduced χ² is 4.3 for rep 1 and
  9–12 for reps 2–4, and the fitted floor is about 0.09 against a readout floor near 0.02.
- An unweighted log-linear fit and a weighted nonlinear fit therefore disagree by
  about 15%. That disagreement is the honest systematic uncertainty. The
  "± 1.1 µs" spread across reps hides it.

### 2.4 Rabi: the estimator cannot resolve the period
- On the 21-point grid, the zero-padded FFT returns **6.187 rad** for true periods
  of both 6.283 and 6.347 rad.
- So "matches the predicted −1.54% to the digit" is a property of the estimator.
  It is not evidence about the hardware.
- Separately, IBM compiles `Rx(θ)` into virtual-Z rotations around fixed √X pulses.
  The sweep varies a frame phase, not the drive amplitude.
- This is a valid end-to-end plumbing test, as OPERATIONS-PLAN framed it. It is not
  a Rabi measurement in BASE's sense.

## 3. The BASE comparison is withdrawn

The frozen script computes BASE's ratio as 50 s divided by an assumed 1 ms "spin-flip
timescale", giving about 5×10⁴. The *Nature* manuscript (Latacz et al., Nature 644,
64–68 (2025)) reports:
- a Rabi frequency of **Ω₀/2π ≈ 50 mHz**, so a π-pulse lasts about 10 s;
- a drive time of **16 s** for the optimized scan;
- an effective coherence time of **50.2(4.8) s**;
- a linewidth of **156(4) mHz FWHM**.

So BASE performs **a few coherent operations per coherence time**, not tens of
thousands. The transmon, at about 10³, is in the *opposite* regime. The claim "same
qualitative regime — the ratio is the analogy's payload" is **retracted**.

BASE's figure of merit is resolution: a narrow linewidth from long coherent
interrogation, not an operation count. Any replacement comparison, such as
Fourier-limited linewidth or fractional frequency resolution, is a new claim. It
should be preregistered before it is published.

The transmon side originally rested on an **assumed** 60 ns gate time. IBM's
calibration records give **24 ns** for `sx` on every qubit used (§8), so the ratio
is about 3.6–3.9×10³ per qubit.

## 6. Root causes, for the next frozen version

1. **The simulation does not test what runs on hardware.** The hardware path rebuilds
   its circuits inline instead of calling `ramsey_circuit` and the other builders, and
   the selftest uses different sweep grids. One set of builders and one set of sweep
   constants should serve both paths.
2. **Estimators were validated only where they cannot fail**: noiseless data at the
   programmed value. Validate across a range of true values on the exact hardware grid,
   including off-nominal frequencies, and add a pass/fail check on T2.
3. **No fit uncertainty or goodness-of-fit output.** Report ±1σ and reduced χ², and
   flag when the model does not fit.
4. **Nothing recorded the transpiler's choices.** Save the physical qubit, the actual
   delays after rounding to the hardware clock, and raw counts (not only P1) at fetch time.

## 11. Primary-source and data-path corrections (2026-10-14)

Two further problems surfaced while drafting the write-up of this repository. Both
were checked against the source before being recorded.

### 11.1 The BASE figures were "corrected" in the wrong direction

`RUN-NIGHT-PREFLIGHT.md` (hash-frozen, not edited) and `REASONING-LOG.md` §2 say the
*Nature* paper reports spin inversion "> 70%" and a linewidth "more than ten times
narrower", and that the "16×" figure was Muse's own arithmetic rather than the paper's
claim. Both statements are wrong.

The abstract of Latacz et al., Nature 644, 64–68 (2025) reports:

- spin-inversion probabilities **greater than 80%** in time-series measurements, at
  spin coherence times of about 50 s;
- inversions **greater than 70%** in scans of single-particle spin resonances;
- transition linewidths **"16 times narrower than in previous measurements"**.

"More than ten times narrower" appears only in the paper's introduction; "16-fold" also
appears in the results and the Figure 4 caption. The original figures (> 80% for the
time series, 16× for the linewidth) were correct for their contexts, and the
correction collapsed two different measurements into one. The preflight table's
"Manuscript (use this)" column should be read with this entry.

None of these figures enters any computed result in this repository.

### 11.2 The retrieval code fitted before saving

In the frozen `fetch()` (`antimatter_analog_ibm.py`), the fit runs before
`np.savetxt` writes the `fetch_<job>_<exp>.csv` file. A fit that raised an error would
have lost that job's export. No fit failed, so all nine files exist, and each was
committed to git before any further analysis. But "saved before analysis" holds only
in that git sense, not in the code path. The files hold measured P1 per sweep point,
not raw shot counts.

For the next frozen version: write counts, job ID, circuit parameters and the physical
qubit to disk before any fit is attempted (§6 item 4).

**Effect on published numbers:** none.

## 12. Analogy wording (2026-10-15)

Public feedback on the write-up noted that parts of this repository describe the BASE
analogy as if it were physical. That is a fair reading of the original text.

**Changed:** the README's mapping table paired "spin-1/2 two-level system" with
"qubit" and "g-factor from precession frequency" with "detuning from Ramsey fringes",
with no label marking the pairing as mathematical. It is now titled "The analogy
(mathematical, not physical)" and lists, for each technique, what BASE does, what this
repo does, and what is actually shared.

**Not changed (frozen):** the docstring of `antimatter_analog_ibm.py` says BASE's
spectroscopy "IS a Rabi experiment" and that the suite is "a calibration of the
measurement chain — the same chain BASE relies on". Both overstate the analogy:

- A transmon is not a spin-½ particle and has no magnetic moment. Its two lowest levels
  obey the same Bloch-sphere equations a spin does; the physics is different.
- BASE drives a real spin with an RF magnetic field. Here the "Rabi" sweep varies a
  compiled gate angle (fixed √X pulses plus software phase), so its 2π period holds by
  construction rather than reflecting a drive calibration.
- The Ramsey detuning is a programmed software phase, not a physical precession
  frequency, and nothing here measures a g-factor or magnetic moment.
- Transmon T1 and T2 are set by dielectric loss, flux noise and quasiparticles; BASE's
  spin coherence is limited by magnetic-field noise in a Penning trap.

What transfers is technique: coherent rotation, Ramsey interferometry, frequency
estimation from fringes, and exponential-decay fitting.

**Effect on published numbers:** none.
